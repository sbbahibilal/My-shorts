# WHAT IF THE INTERNET WENT OFF? — PART 2: THE RULES. Fiction, part of a series. Narrator: Kokoro am_michael.
# One line = one clause block. [beat 1.0] = real silence. [Glitch] = the unknown sender (low whisper). [Neighbor] = upstairs neighbor.
Day thirty one. The world learned to whisper.
The buildings stood there like tall unanswered questions.
Cold toast. Cold coffee. A phone that shouldn't work.
[beat 1.0]
Then it buzzed.
[Glitch] New rules. You'll want to break them.
No one was typing. No one was there.
The rules came one by one, like footsteps on stairs.
[beat 1.0]
One.
Two buckets a day, boiled before you trust them.
Clean water was about to become the only money.
Two.
Cash for bread. Cards already lied.
Three.
Sleep in shifts. Someone always watches the stairwell.
Four.
Chalk mark on the door when you're safe.
The hallway built a quiet country out of chalk marks.
[beat 1.0]
Then, at nine, it changed its mind.
[Glitch] Good. Water stops at noon. All of it.
We laughed. Water is pipes.
At one minute to twelve, the pipes sang like kettles.
At noon, the song stopped.
[beat 1.5]
The tap gave nothing. Not water, not air.
Then the ceiling knocked. Twice.
[Neighbor] Tell me your phone buzzed too.
Two phones on the table. One message. Same second. Same length.
The city had a rulebook, and its author was watching us read it.
[beat 1.2]
[Glitch] The tower on Fourth still has water. For now.
Dear phone. That is not a hint. That is a leash.
Four blocks. No power. And a stranger who knows my floors.
[beat 1.0]
We packed the bags before we finished deciding.
The stairwell smelled like a question all the way down.
It knows when the water stops. What else does it know?
