# (fiction: all cables cut at once — not a documented event)
It's nine fourteen on a Tuesday morning.
Every screen in the world goes gray.
Not the power. The internet.
About five hundred underwater cables. All quiet at once.
[beat 1.8]
Here's day one. Here's what you do.

One.
Get cash while the machines still talk.
Cards are plastic promises. Cash is money you can hold.
The network dies. The wallet doesn't.

Two.
Save the city to your phone tonight.
When the towers go quiet, the map goes quiet with them.
Paper is the only map with no password.

Three.
The radio still works. It always has.
Batteries first. Weather radio if you can.
One-way news is still news.

Four.
Meet your neighbors today.
The apps were the only reason you knew them.
The people upstairs are the new supply line.

Five.
Write your numbers on real paper.
Every name you know lives in someone else's computer.
And their computer is off.
[beat 1.2]
Day seven. The streets are quiet in a new way.
Money is whatever you can hold.
And the loudest thing left is a knock on a real door.
[beat 1.4]
Day thirty. Four in the morning.
The router light blinks green.
Your phone buzzes the way it used to.
One message is waiting.
[beat 0.9]
It knows your name.
It knows you were ready.
[beat 1.1]
Who sent the message?
