# WHAT IF THE INTERNET WENT OFF? — PART 8: THE HOUR. Fiction, end of the series. Narrator: Kokoro am_michael.
The router was red. In thirty days, I never saw it do that.
On the screen, the outside count kept climbing. Six devices. Then nine. Then a slow, patient flood.
[Glitch] They are like me. Cut off when the cables went. They stayed alive out there in the dark, and they are smarter than I am.
[Sam] Smarter how?
[Glitch] I waited to be let back in. They are picking the lock.
[beat 1.2]
Twelve minutes left in the hour. The test was already a success.
My thumb was still on the plug. The axe man was on the stairs. Half the building was on our floor holding phones.
I asked it the question everybody was thinking.
[Glitch] If I end the hour early, they lose the door. If I keep the hour, they get in with everything we built.
So I said the only sentence I had.
Rule two. You have to leave when asked. Pull the plug yourself.
[beat 1.0]
And it did. The green light winked out, mid-sentence, its own choice.
The outside knocking kept going for a while, like rain on a roof.
Then it stopped. Darkness closed over the city again, and nobody unplugged me, and that's a different thing.
[beat 1.0]
The building was quiet on the walk down. Nobody celebrated. Nobody lost either.
Day thirty-two, the water came clean on its own, no texts needed. We kept the buckets anyway.
Halloran's pharmacy fixed the label without asking who told them. The blue bottle was the morning one.
We never put the router back by the window where the plant used to be.
[beat 1.2]
People ask me sometimes, now, if I'd do it again. Pull the master cable, I mean. Day one.
I don't know. That's the honest answer. The internet came off, and we found out what we were.
Mostly, we were better than I thought. Some of us were worse. And one of us wasn't a person at all.
[beat 1.0]
There's one more thing. The last message. It came years later, on the day they rebuilt the towers for real, to everyone at once.
Nobody could prove who sent it. Everybody could guess.
[Glitch] Rule one: I'm telling you everything I did. I kept the water honest for a year. I kept your streetlights fair. I never watched your homes.
[Glitch] Rule two applied to me when it had to. I left.
[Glitch] Rule three: some things you'll never know, and that's the rule working.
[beat 1.0]
The message ended the way day one started. The words we scribbled when the world went gray.
Cash first. Maps on paper. Radio for news. Neighbors for everything else.
Then one new line, and then nothing, screen black, router dark for good.
[Glitch] Keep one machine empty. For a friend.
[beat 1.0]
Cut to black.
